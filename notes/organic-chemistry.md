# Organic Chemistry — Key Concepts

## Functional Groups
Functional groups are specific atoms or groups of atoms that determine
the chemical properties and reactions of a molecule.

| Group        | Formula | Example         |
|--------------|---------|-----------------|
| Hydroxyl     | -OH     | Ethanol (C₂H₅OH)|
| Carbonyl     | C=O     | Acetone          |
| Carboxyl     | -COOH   | Acetic acid      |
| Amino        | -NH₂    | Glycine (amino acid) |
| Ester        | -COO-   | Fats/oils        |

## Types of Reactions

### Substitution Reaction
One atom or group is replaced by another.
- SN1: two-step, carbocation intermediate, favored by 3° carbon
- SN2: one-step, backside attack, favored by 1° carbon

### Addition Reaction
Two reactants combine to form a single product (double bond → single bond).
**Example:** CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃-CH₃  (hydrogenation)

### Elimination Reaction
Atoms are removed from adjacent carbons, forming a double bond.
**Example:** CH₃CH₂Br + KOH → CH₂=CH₂ + KBr + H₂O

## Isomers
Molecules with the same molecular formula but different structures.
- **Structural isomers:** differ in connectivity
- **Stereoisomers:** same connectivity, different spatial arrangement
  - Enantiomers: non-superimposable mirror images (chiral molecules)
  - Diastereomers: stereoisomers that are not mirror images

## IUPAC Naming
1. Find the longest carbon chain → parent name (methane, ethane, propane…)
2. Number carbons to give substituents lowest numbers
3. Name substituents as prefixes (methyl-, ethyl-, bromo-…)
4. Add suffixes for functional groups (-ol, -al, -one, -oic acid…)
